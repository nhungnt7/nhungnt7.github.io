When Intelligent Agents Talk, Can Their Biases Become Stronger?

Paper: The Social Cost of Intelligence: Emergence, Propagation, and Amplification of Stereotypical Bias in Multi-Agent Systems

Sep. 2026 · 6 min read · AACL-IJCNLP 2026 Findings

TL;DR. This work asks how stereotypical bias behaves when multiple intelligent agents interact—not only whether one model displays bias in isolation. We study three connected processes: emergence, propagation, and amplification.

Why look beyond a single model?

Many evaluations study one language model at a time. Agentic systems introduce a different setting: agents exchange messages, respond to one another, and repeatedly influence future decisions.

A behavior that appears limited in one agent may therefore develop differently once it enters a group. Understanding the collective—not only its individual members—is important for building safer agentic systems.

Three dynamics to understand

Emergence

Can group interaction produce biased behavior that was not obvious at the individual level?

Propagation

How can a biased signal travel from one agent to others through communication?

Amplification

Can repeated interaction make stereotypical bias stronger over time?

Why this matters

As LLM-based agents move toward collaborative planning and large-scale coordination, safety evaluation must account for collective behavior. A safe individual agent does not automatically guarantee a safe society of agents.
